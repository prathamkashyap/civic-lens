Spelling & grammar check

No spelling errors found in the main text.
Page 3, Table I caption: Missing period at end of caption sentence — minor but inconsistent with other captions.
Page 4, Section IV-B: "exhibits higher variance (±0.046 vs. ±0.012), suggesting instability in capturing…" — grammatically fine but "instability" is too strong; "greater sensitivity" is more accurate.
Table V: Clusters 2, 3, 4 show "–" in Pending% column — this looks like missing data. Either fill the values or add a footnote explaining why they're omitted.
Equation label mismatch: Equation (2) uses variable r = 0.003° but the text in Section III-F says ε = 0.002° for DBSCAN — these are different parameters but the notation overlaps with DBSCAN's epsilon symbol used in other spatial ML papers. Clarify notation distinctly.

Technical & content issues

Critical — synthetic data only: The entire evaluation uses a self-generated 1,600-record dataset. No real-world validation means reviewers at most venues will reject on generalizability grounds. This must be addressed.
Arbitrary scoring weights: The label function uses weights 0.4, 0.3, 0.3 with no justification or sensitivity analysis. If these weights change even slightly, priority labels — and therefore all results — shift substantially.
Weak baseline selection: Only two models are compared (Decision Tree vs Logistic Regression). Not including Random Forest, XGBoost, or even k-NN means the result that "tree-based > linear" is unsurprising and understated.
Ablation trade-off not discussed: Table IV shows High Recall drops from 0.88 → 0.83 when density is added. This regression is never mentioned in the discussion — it should be addressed honestly.
No statistical significance test: Cross-validation results are reported as mean ± std but no t-test or Wilcoxon test confirms the decision tree's improvement over logistic regression is statistically significant.
School affiliation inconsistency: Dr. Kesarwany is listed under SASL (Applied Sciences & Language), but this seems inconsistent for a computing paper. Verify the affiliation is correct for submission.
Circular dependency concern is acknowledged and addressed well in Section III-C.
Ablation study design is methodologically sound and adds real value to the paper.
